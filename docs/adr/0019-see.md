# ADR 0019: Static exchange evaluation, and a prediction that held

**Date:** 2026-09-25
**Status:** accepted. **+29.1 Elo**, H1 after 538 games, bounds [0, 25].

## Why this one was built next

ADR 0018 found that the six search features measured so far split cleanly: the
three that gained bet on **move ordering**, and the three that lost bet on the
**evaluation**.

That is an observation, and an observation that only explains the past is worth
very little. So it was turned into a prediction. SEE reads material off the
board and never asks the evaluation function anything, which puts it squarely in
the group that works. **ADR 0018 predicted SEE would gain.**

It gained +29.1 Elo, H1 accepted, on a run that audits clean. The prediction
held, which is the first evidence that the pattern in ADR 0018 is a mechanism
rather than a coincidence over six samples.

## What SEE does that MVV-LVA cannot

MVV-LVA sorts captures by "how big is the victim, how small is the attacker".
That is a guess about the first move of an exchange and nothing else. It cannot
see that RxP is losing when the pawn is defended, so the rook capture is tried
first, the refutation is searched, and the work is discarded.

SEE plays the exchange out: both sides keep capturing on the square with their
cheapest attacker, and each side may stop whenever continuing is worse than
standing pat. Two uses:

1. **Ordering.** A capture SEE says loses material is ranked *below quiet moves*,
   not merely last among the captures. A losing capture really is worse than an
   ordinary quiet move.
2. **Quiescence.** A losing capture is skipped entirely. Delta pruning asks
   whether a capture is big enough; this asks whether it is sound, which is a
   question the stand-pat score cannot answer.

## Two bugs the hand-computed tests caught

**The fold-back was one step short.** The counter has to advance once per
capture *including the original move*, with the gain computed before the next
attacker is sought. Written the other way round, a single recapture is never
folded in, so every defended capture read as free: pawn-takes-defended-pawn
returned +100 instead of 0, and rook-takes-defended-pawn returned +100 instead
of -400.

**The conventional early exit misfires with this indexing.** The usual "both
options are losing, so stop" test cut the loop before a second rook could
recapture, returning -400 on doubled rooks against a defended pawn where the
exchange is -300. It is a speed optimization on a loop that runs a handful of
times, so it was removed rather than debugged.

One test also failed because the *position* was wrong, not the code: a knight on
d2 does not attack d5.

## What this does not claim

That SEE is exact. It ignores pins, so a defender that cannot legally recapture
is still counted, and promotions are approximated by adding the promoted piece's
value minus a pawn. Both are the conventional simplifications. The king is given
a value that dwarfs everything, so a king capture into a still-defended square
falls out of the arithmetic instead of needing a special case.

Four for four on ordering, nought for three on evaluation. The ordering-dependent
side of the search is now largely built, and what remains on the list is the side
that cannot pay until the evaluation improves.
